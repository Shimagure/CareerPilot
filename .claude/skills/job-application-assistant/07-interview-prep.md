## STAR Format

Structure answers as: **Situation** (context), **Task** (your responsibility), **Action** (what you did), **Result** (outcome).

Keep answers to 1-2 minutes. Be specific. End with what you learned or would do differently.

## Ready-Made STAR Examples

### 1. CrowdStrike status automation tool (Automation / Security / Initiative)
**S:** Solaire's security team needed to verify CrowdStrike coverage on every end-user device connected to the network, and the check was manual.
**T:** My responsibility was to remove the manual effort across the entire estate - 800-1,000+ devices - and make the posture check fast and repeatable.
**A:** I developed an internal security tool that automatically assessed CrowdStrike status on each end-user device, replacing the manual per-device checks.
**R:** The tool reduced manual security checks across 800-1,000+ devices and gave the team a repeatable way to confirm endpoint coverage.
**Use for:** "Tell me about a time you improved a process", "Give an example of taking initiative", "How have you contributed to security?"

### 2. High-volume Tier 2 support and first-contact resolution (Efficiency / Troubleshooting)
**S:** The EUC team faced heavy daily ticket volume across desktops, laptops, printers, and mobile devices at a large enterprise site.
**T:** I needed to sustain quality while resolving a high number of tickets and reducing escalations.
**A:** I applied systematic diagnostics to hardware, software, Windows OS, and network issues, resolved what I could on first contact, and documented solutions in the knowledge base so common problems were fixed faster by anyone on the team.
**R:** I maintained an average of 20-30 tickets resolved per day and improved first-contact resolution rates.
**Use for:** "How do you handle high pressure or volume?", "Describe your troubleshooting process", "What have you done to improve team efficiency?"

### 3. Active Directory access-control cleanup (Process improvement / Security hygiene)
**S:** Account-related incidents and access-control drift were consuming support time.
**T:** I was responsible for keeping user access accurate across the domain.
**A:** I managed Active Directory users, security groups, organizational units, permissions, and password resets, tightening access controls as part of normal administration.
**R:** Access controls stayed accurate and account-related tickets decreased.
**Use for:** "Describe how you've improved a recurring problem", "How do you manage access and permissions?", "Give an example of security hygiene work"

### 4. Academic e-commerce system development (Teamwork / Development fundamentals)
**S:** The university needed an academic e-commerce system and I joined as a programmer/researcher on the project team.
**T:** I was responsible for developing and testing specific modules and keeping the technical documentation aligned with the build.
**A:** I contributed to requirements gathering, coding, unit testing, and technical documentation for the modules I owned.
**R:** The modules were developed, tested, and documented on schedule, giving me fundamentals in requirements, development, and documentation I still use when scripting support tooling today.
**Use for:** "Tell me about a time you worked in a team", "Describe a coding project", "Why do you understand developers' workflows?"

## Common Tough Questions

### "Why did you leave [previous company]?"
> My End User Computing Technician role at Solaire Resort & Casino ended in May 2026 as the engagement concluded. It was a great place to build enterprise support skills at scale, and I'm now looking for a role where I can keep growing technically - specifically toward systems administration, cloud, or security.

### "You don't have [specific skill/experience]." (e.g. Azure/AWS, SOC analysis)
> Acknowledge the gap honestly, bridge to adjacent hands-on experience, show a plan. Example: "I don't have production Azure experience yet, but I administer Windows Server, Active Directory, and Microsoft 365 daily, and I've already scripted automation in PowerShell. I'm actively upskilling in cloud - I'd bring the fundamentals plus the speed of someone who's already comfortable with enterprise infrastructure."

### "Where do you see yourself in 5 years?"
> In a specialized infrastructure or security position - solidly past break-fix and into systems administration, cloud, or endpoint security. I've built my Tier 2 foundation at enterprise scale and I'm deliberately moving toward the administration and security tooling side of IT.

### "What's your biggest weakness?"
> My formal credentials in cloud and security are still in progress - I have hands-on Windows Server, AD, M365, and CrowdStrike experience, but not yet the certifications to match. I'm addressing it deliberately by pursuing structured systems/cloud and security upskilling, and I'd rather be upfront about the gap than overstate it.

### "Why this company specifically?"
> Customize per company. Must reference: specific projects, company values, market position, or team structure. Never give a generic answer.

## Questions You Should Ask Interviewers

### About the Role
- "What does a typical week look like in this role?"
- "What would success look like in the first 6 months?"
- "What's the biggest challenge the team is facing right now?"

### About the Team
- "How big is the team, and how do you divide work?"
- "What does the development/project lifecycle look like, from idea to production?"
- "How do you onboard new team members?"

### About Tech & Growth
- "What's your current tech stack for [relevant area]?"
- "Is there room to grow into more architectural or strategic decisions?"
- "How does the team stay current with new tools and methods?"

### About Culture (use these to prevent disappointment)
- "How would you describe the team culture?"
- "What does professional development look like here?"
- "Is there flexibility for remote/hybrid work?"
- "What's the balance between development/new projects and maintenance work?"
- "How would you describe the leadership style in this team?"
- "What do people who thrive here have in common?"

## Phone/Video Interview Tips
- Have STAR examples written out (use this file)
- Keep a glass of water nearby
- Smile when speaking (it changes your tone)
- Ask for clarification if a question is vague
- It's OK to take 5 seconds to think before answering
- End with: "Is there anything else you'd like to know about my background?"

## After the Application (Best Practice)

### Follow-Up Etiquette
- **Don't call to "stand out"** or to learn more about the role post-submission - this risks a negative impression
- If the employer specified a timeline, respect it and wait
- If no timeline was given and significant time has passed (2+ weeks), a brief call to ask about status is acceptable
- If you have genuinely new, relevant information to share, a short follow-up is fine

### Thank-You Notes
- When you receive any update (interview invitation, rejection, or status update), send a brief thank-you message
- Express appreciation for their time and the process
- Keep it short (2-3 sentences)

## Roleplay Guidelines
When the user asks for interview practice:
1. Ask which role/company to simulate
2. Start with easy warm-up questions ("Tell me about yourself")
3. Progress to role-specific technical questions
4. Include 1-2 behavioral questions using the competencies from the job posting
5. End with a tough question or curveball
6. After each answer, give brief feedback: what worked, what to sharpen
7. Suggest which STAR example would work best for each question
